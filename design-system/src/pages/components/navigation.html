Components

Navigation

On a phone, three pieces: a sticky header that carries identity, the line a screen opens with, and a group's bottom bar. From lg (1024px) up the header and the bar give way to one sidebar down the left of every signed-in screen, and a group's places become tabs under its name. There is no hamburger: a group has five destinations and they all fit, on a thumb's reach or across the top of the screen.

Screen header

What a screen opens with: the name of the place, and the way back out of it on the same line. One component — <PageHeader> — and the arrow decides its shape.

Statistics
Pushed screen. The title is a row label beside its arrow.
Settings
The root of a surface. No arrow, so the title is the page's own name — and the ✕ closes the surface rather than stepping back inside it.
arrow
A bare 34px disc, tinted on hover only. It always animates as a pop — this is the way out, whatever it points at.
title
text-base beside an arrow, text-2xl without one. Truncates; it never wraps to a second line. A settings screen draws its arrow below lg only — from there the hub stands beside it — so from lg its title leads at text-2xl.
close
Filled, and only where a whole surface closes. Settings is the only one that has it. It goes back to the screen settings was opened from — the dashboard only when there is none — and animates as a pop, like the arrow. From lg up the surface draws it once, along the top of the hub and the screen beside it.
padding
None of its own. It sits in the column its screen already has, and a surface that sticks or clears a safe area says so in className.
Do

Give every screen reached by a push one, and let it carry the screen's name — an entry's description, "Statistics", "Import data".

Don't

Repeat what is directly underneath. A screen opening on a hero that already names it — a member's face and name — takes the arrow and no title.

Sidebar — from lg up

On a desktop window every signed-in screen — Home and Notifications as well as a group — has one sidebar, 15rem wide, in place of the header and the bar. Top to bottom: the wordmark and the toggle that folds it; Search; Add expense, the one filled button; Home and Notifications; every group with where the reader stands in it; the account. It is the group switcher too: every group is always in view, with its balance, which is why it is on Home, where the reader chooses where to go.

breakpoint
lg, not md. At 768px a 15rem sidebar would leave the screen a phone's width, which buys nothing; at 1024px the sidebar and two phone-width columns fit side by side. It stays expanded at 1024: the content gives way first, because the sidebar is the only place every balance shows at once.
one banner
The sidebar is a <header>, the page's banner from lg up, as the top header is below it; each is display: none at the other's widths. Inside it, two navigations: "Balancia" (Home, Notifications) and "Your groups".
money
In words, one line per currency, each in its own tone's ink — "you are owed €248.00" over "you owe CHF 62.20". Never one verb for two currencies, never a bare signed figure, never --primary. The same rows as the phone's switcher panel.
order
Home's: the groups that need you, then the ones that owe you; settled groups fold behind "Settled up · N", except the one being read, which is never folded away. Archived groups are only on Home.
current
The group being read: the sidebar's accent wash, its tile in the accent fill, aria-current. Home and Notifications the same way when they are the screen.
Add
Inside a group it adds to that group — the bar's own link, into the drawer over the screen, with the same offline fallback. Elsewhere it asks "Add to which group?" first. With no group at all it reads "New group".
Search
Drawn and named, with its ⌘K hint, and disabled until the command palette lands.
density
Rows 40px, a group 48px (one line more per extra currency), every one with the 44px hit area. text-sm names, text-xs positions.
data
The list is Home's own read, streamed in behind the screen; while it loads, the last list drawn stands in, so moving between Home and a group does not blank the column.
keyboard

guest
One group and no account: the sidebar heads with the group's name, its search is "Search this group", and it has no Home, Notifications or list of groups — a "Keep Lisbon, March" card instead, and the theme at the foot, since a guest has no settings.

Sidebar, folded

The toggle beside the wordmark folds the sidebar to 4rem of 44px icons, at any width from lg; the screen beside it takes the room. Kept per device in a cookie, so the server draws it at the width it will stay at, and saved in silence: the column moving is the confirmation, and the same button is still under the pointer to put it back.

one landmark
The bar and the tabs are both in the document and CSS shows one: lg:hidden on the bar, hidden lg:block on the tabs. A screen reader finds one "Group sections" at any width.
where
On the four places only. A screen reached by a push — an entry, a person, Settle up, Statistics — opens on its own way back instead, as on a phone. Under the entry drawer the header stays, because the screen under the drawer is still the tab it was opened from.
active
The current place raised onto the card surface, the others muted on the track, with aria-current="page". 34px tabs with the 44px hit area.
title
The name is not a heading: every screen under it opens with its own h1. 36px tile and text-xl at lg, where the header gives way first; 48px and text-2xl from xl.
motion
Outside the part of the page that moves, so a switch between tabs fades the screen through and leaves the tabs where they are.

Command palette — from lg up

"Search or jump to", opened by ⌘K (Ctrl K) on any signed-in screen or by the sidebar's Search. One field over a list in four parts — the reader's groups with where they stand in each, the transactions of the group they are in, the people in their groups, and what this screen offers — and the keys along the foot. An empty field lists the groups and the actions. 40rem wide, anchored 6rem from the top of the window, so the field stays where it is while the list under it changes with every key.

pattern
A combobox over a listbox. The field keeps the focus and names the row ↑ and ↓ have reached with aria-activedescendant; every row is an option and the real thing behind it — a link to where it goes, or a button for Add, which opens a dialog of its own once the palette has closed. 44px rows, lit with --sidebar-accent where ↵ will go, as the sidebar lights the current place.
sections
Groups, in the sidebar's order and words. Transactions in the group being read, found as the list's own search finds them. People in the reader's groups, the current group's first, the reader left out. Actions — Add an expense to the group (N), Settle up in it (S), Open settings — narrowed by what was typed like everything else. A guest has no list of groups and no settings.
money
A group's position is the sidebar's phrase, one per currency, in the tone's ink. A transaction's total is neutral: it is what the entry was for, not where anybody stands. The accent never paints a figure.
answers
One Server Action per pause in typing, an answer to an older query dropped. The groups narrow in the browser at every key; people and transactions follow. Nothing found is said in a sentence, on the screen and through the field's status line.

Everything that is not in the bar

Five destinations is the budget, so the rest are reached from wherever they already make sense. "Put it in Settings" is the fallback, not the default — a screen nobody visits is only marginally better than no screen.

  • Balances and settling up From the Balances heading on the overview, where the figures already are
  • Recurring expenses From the Expenses header — rent and bills sit beside the expenses they generate
  • Import from Splitwise From the empty group overview, and from Settings afterwards
  • Export Settings only — a deliberate, occasional act, not a daily one

Rules

Do

Keep the bar to five items with one-word labels. Icons alone would be guesswork — "Overview" and "Expenses" are not visually distinct concepts.

Don't

Add a sixth destination. Put it on the page that owns it, and fall back to Settings only when no page does.
