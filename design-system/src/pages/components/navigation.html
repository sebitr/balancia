Components

Navigation

Three pieces only: a sticky header that carries identity, the line a screen opens with, and a group's navigation — a bottom bar on a phone, and from lg (1024px) up a rail down the left of the window. There is no hamburger: a group has five destinations and they all fit, on a thumb's reach or down the side.

Screen header

What a screen opens with: the name of the place, and the way back out of it on the same line. One component — <PageHeader> — and the arrow decides its shape.

Statistics
Pushed screen. The title is a row label beside its arrow.
Settings
The root of a surface. No arrow, so the title is the page's own name — and the ✕ closes the surface rather than stepping back inside it.
arrow
A bare 34px disc, tinted on hover only. It always animates as a pop — this is the way out, whatever it points at.
title
text-base beside an arrow, text-2xl without one. Truncates; it never wraps to a second line.
close
Filled, and only where a whole surface closes. Settings is the only one that has it. It goes back to the screen settings was opened from — the dashboard only when there is none — and animates as a pop, like the arrow.
padding
None of its own. It sits in the column its screen already has, and a surface that sticks or clears a safe area says so in className.
Do

Give every screen reached by a push one, and let it carry the screen's name — an entry's description, "Statistics", "Import data".

Don't

Repeat what is directly underneath. A screen opening on a hero that already names it — a member's face and name — takes the arrow and no title.

Group rail — from lg up

On a desktop the header and the bar fold into one column down the left of the window: the switcher at the top, Add and the four places in the middle, the bell and the account at the foot. The same controls, in the same document order, so Tab walks the rail top to bottom and then the screen.

breakpoint
lg, not md. At 768px a 15rem rail would leave the screen a phone's width, which buys nothing; at 1024px the rail and two phone-width columns fit side by side.
one landmark
The bar and the rail are both in the document and CSS shows one: lg:hidden on the bar, hidden lg:flex on the rail. A screen reader finds one "Group sections" at any width, with the same five names.
Add
The rail's one filled button, at its head. The same link, the same name and the same offline fallback as the bar's disc.
active
The bar's two cues, unchanged: --primary-ink label and an accent disc behind the icon, with aria-current="page".
density
40px rows rather than 74px tabs — a pointer is not a thumb. Type stays on the desk scale, text-sm.
keyboard

switcher
Its panel takes a 20rem width of its own and opens out over the screen; the rail is narrower than the rows it lists.

Everything that is not in the bar

Five destinations is the budget, so the rest are reached from wherever they already make sense. "Put it in Settings" is the fallback, not the default — a screen nobody visits is only marginally better than no screen.

  • Balances and settling up From the Balances heading on the overview, where the figures already are
  • Recurring expenses From the Expenses header — rent and bills sit beside the expenses they generate
  • Import from Splitwise From the empty group overview, and from Settings afterwards
  • Export Settings only — a deliberate, occasional act, not a daily one

Rules

Do

Keep the bar to five items with one-word labels. Icons alone would be guesswork — "Overview" and "Expenses" are not visually distinct concepts.

Don't

Add a sixth destination. Put it on the page that owns it, and fall back to Settings only when no page does.
