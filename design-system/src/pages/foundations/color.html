Foundations

Colour

Every colour is an OKLCH token defined in src/app/globals.css and consumed through a semantic name — never a raw value in a component. Light and dark are two sets of the same names; toggle the theme to check both.

All pairings meet WCAG 2.2 AA on their intended background in both themes. Re-check any new pairing before shipping it — the OKLCH lightness values were chosen for this, not by eye.

Charts

chart-1 chart-2 chart-3 chart-4 chart-5

Plum, the accent, teal, slate, then plum 300 — categorical only, ordered by first use. --chart-2 is var(--primary), because it is the "you" series; the other four are kept a visible distance from every accent and every money colour, so a bar is never mistaken for a balance. Chart colours are not balance colours: never reuse --chart-3 to mean "positive" or --chart-2 to mean "owes".

Accent

The reader picks one of seven accents on the appearance screen. Each is a seed — lightness, chroma, hue — in src/modules/profile/accent.ts, from which the app derives a --primary-ink per theme, walked to 4.5:1. Six variables in all, and the money colours are not among them: green means the same thing on every account. Three of the seven do sit near a money colour — coral by the “you owe” red, mint on the green, amber on the payer — and that is accepted, because a balance is told apart by its sign and its word rather than by its hue.

coral amber mint ocean lavender raspberry plum

Coral is two degrees from the "you owe" red and mint is the "gets back" green — so under those accents the money colour moves, not the accent: ruby for "you owe" under coral, grass for "gets back" under mint, olive for the payer under amber. The other four leave all three where they are. This kit shows the palette as drawn, with the accent unpainted; the app paints the seed inline on <html>, and the tokens above read it through var(--accent-…, fallback).

Do: bg-primary on the button, the ring, the link, the "you" pill and the "you" series in a chart. Don't: --primary on an amount, a balance bar or the chip above a figure — those take their tone from TONE in src/components/money/balance-tone.ts.

Surfaces and contrast

The theme decides light or dark. Cream is the light palette and is not a choice; at night the reader picks Plum or Midnight, an override block at the end of globals.css applied when <html> carries data-dark="midnight". Increased contrast is not a choice either — it follows prefers-contrast: more from a media query, and darkens captions, strengthens lines and takes every ink from 4.5:1 to 7:1.

cream plum midnight

One ordering rule is left: the dark contrast block is written :root.dark rather than .dark, so it does not lose the specificity tie against Midnight, and the media query it sits in comes last. src/app/token-contrast.test.ts checks every ink in all six combinations.
