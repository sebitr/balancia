Patterns

Expense timeline

Expenses and settlements share a single chronological list, because that is how people remember a trip — not as two separate ledgers. Settlements stay visually distinct: they are repayments, not spending.

Row grammar

title
The expense description, or who paid whom for a repayment — from the reader's side when they are one of the two: "You paid Marta back", "Sam paid you back", and "Sam paid Marta" only between two others. The repayment's own screen and the search use the same sentence. Truncates before the badges do.
your line
Under the total, what the row left the reader holding, as a sentence in text-xs: "You get back €60.00", "You owe €30.00", "You paid your share". On a repayment, which way the money went — "You received €30.00", "You paid €30.00" — in the neutral ink. No sign: the words carry the direction, the colour comes from TONE. Nothing at all on a row the reader is not in.
Repayment badge
outline — marks the row as a repayment rather than a cost.
Recurring badge
secondary — this row was generated from a rule.
metadata
date · payers · N receipts, middle-dot separated, text-xs muted. Receipts only appear when there are some.
amount
Right, tabular, font-medium. Plain Amount — a cost has no direction, so it takes no balance tone. The reader's line sits under it, never in place of it.
sort
By expense date descending, then by creation time — so two things dated the same day appear in the order they were entered.

Multiple payers are joined with commas: an expense can be split across people who paid, not just people who owe. Mixed currencies live in the same list and each row shows its own — the list is a history, not a total.

Navigability

Do

Make every row an anchor to its own screen — the whole row, so the tap target is 44px-plus on a phone. A repayment has one too: the one thing its row cannot say is whether it finished the job, and that is what its screen is for.

Don't

Split the two into separate lists or separate tabs. "Did we pay that back yet?" is answered by seeing the repayment sitting next to the expense.

At width — from lg up

From 1024px the same rows are drawn as a table, and the chips, the category spine and the search row give way to one row of filters. It is not a second list: one loader, one filter in the URL, one paging hook, and the table is handed exactly the rows the phone's list would draw. Below lg only the list is mounted and nothing moves.

columns
Date · Description · Category · Paid by · Split · Amount · For you. Decided by the table's own box: under 60rem the middle three fold into the description's second line and the reader's line sits under the amount; For you stands alone from 68rem. The description keeps about 290px either side of each step.
date
Day and month; the year only on a row from another year. Sorted newest first by default, and the header says so with aria-sort and an arrow. Pressing it turns the list round; Amount ranks largest first where the group's amounts share one currency.
paid by
Avatar and name, "You" for the reader in the accent's "you" pill, two payers joined the way the language joins them.
split
"6 equally" when everyone carries a share, "4 of 6" when some do, the method beside it when the split was not even. A repayment is not shared out: its split is how it was paid, when somebody said.
amount, for you
The total plain, as in the list; the reader's line in its tone's words, never a sign. Nothing on a row the reader is not in.
footer
"Showing 40 of 42 transactions · sorted by date, newest first". Rows arrive a page at a time as the window scrolls, exactly as on a phone.
filters
The kind chips stay chips — several may be on, which a segmented control would say cannot happen. Category and When are menus that apply as they are used; a month is written as the custom range the sheet's date fields then show. More filters opens the phone's sheet for the rest, its badge counting only what the row cannot show. The category spine is not drawn at width: the Category column says the same thing row by row.
