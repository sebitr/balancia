Joining from a group link

A group's link is the same for everybody it was sent to, so it cannot know who opened it. It opens on the one question that follows from that — which of these names is you — and then asks only how to come in. Two screens, then the group itself. It reads as an invitation, and once the reader has said who they are, every balance is said to them as theirs.

Choosing "Create an account" or "I already have an account" adds the email-and-code screen between the second screen and the group, and nothing else. The progress bar measures the route the reader is on, so it sits at half on the second screen until a guest's tap ends the route.

A personal invitation ends the same way for a guest: their name, then the group, with the same toast and the same guest card. An account taken from a personal invitation, or created from /register by a guest, still gets "You're in" and the setup checklist, because it can save every row of it — but that screen's buttons say what they do: "Finish setting up" opens the checklist, and "Go to the group" sits under it. No button says "See the group" and opens something else.

The second screen, three ways

  • Signed out, a listed name "Alex, how do you want to join Flat share?" — Create an account · I already have an account · Continue as a guest. The guest option is the join itself.
  • Signed in, a listed name "Alex, ready to join Flat share?" — "You're signed in as Léa Martin. Your balance and the 2 expenses under your name go on that account." One button: Join Flat share.
  • Nobody on the list ("None of these") "What should Flat share call you?" — a name field first, then the same ways in, each waiting for the name, and "Back to the list" under them.

What the copy has to carry

  • "Sam invited you to Flat share. Which of these is you?" An invitation, from the person whose name the link's own chat preview already shows — nothing a link-holder could not learn before tapping. Without a name: "You're invited to Flat share."
  • "owes €60.00 · 2 expenses" The word and the amount together, in the balance's tone, never "filed" and never a bare signed figure.
  • "You owe €60.00" From the moment they have said who they are, the balance is theirs and the sentence says "you". The word carries the direction, so the figure goes unsigned.
  • "Nothing you add is lost if you create an account later." "Create an account", not "claim". The reader never meets the word for what the server does to a seat.
  • "…and lets you say how to be paid." On the guest card, only for somebody who gets money back. Somebody who owes does not need a payout method to pay.

Rules

Do

Let a button do what it says. The join ends in the group, with a toast saying "you're in"; nothing stands between them.

Don't

Commit on the tap that picks a row. Picking only opens the next screen, which names the person and keeps "Not you? Back to the list" under its buttons.

Do

Name the signed-in account on the list, before anybody picks: a link opened on a borrowed laptop is how a balance gets taken by the wrong person.

Don't

List a person's expenses at their full price under their balance. The lines would never add up to it; the list already shows the balance and the count.
