Patterns

Money

The most important pattern in the product, and the one with the least room for interpretation. A balance always carries a word, an icon, and a colour — three redundant cues, so the meaning survives greyscale, colour blindness, and a screen reader.

Amount — a plain quantity

€128.40 CHF 31.20 1 240 SEK ¥18,400

<Amount> renders a quantity with no direction: the cost of an expense, a total, a share. Minor units arrive as a decimal string and go through Intl.NumberFormat, which is why the symbol placement, separators, and decimal count differ above — yen has no minor unit, and Swedish krona puts the code last. Never hand-assemble a currency string.

The magnitude is always rendered unsigned. Direction is the arrow and the word; a minus sign on top of them is a third encoding of the same fact and reads as a typo. When showLabel is false the word moves to sr-only rather than disappearing — it is never actually removed.

Hiding the word is for a figure whose row has no room left and already carries an arrow — the per-currency header, beside the spend. It is not for a list: a name followed by a red figure is a colour doing the explaining. A figure with no arrow and no visible word keeps its sign.

One arrow pair

in — coming to whoever the line is about out — going from them

ArrowDownLeft and ArrowUpRight, and no other direction glyph, on every screen that draws one: the dashboard, the group's hero, the per-currency list, a person's page, the statistics' flows. Straight ArrowUp / ArrowDown are gone — the group's hero pointed up for "you owe" while a person's page pointed down for the same debt, so one figure turned its arrow over between two screens. Where a word now sits beside the figure the glyph may be dropped altogether; it is never swapped for another.

Said to the reader

a balance
"Sam / owes" · "You / get back" — the name, and the verb under it in the muted label style. The reader is "You", never their own name.
an entry
"You get back €60.00" · "You owe €30.00" · "You paid your share" — a line of its own, so it starts with a capital, in the tone's ink.
a repayment
"You received €30.00" · "You paid €30.00" — which way the money went, in the neutral ink: a repayment closes a position rather than opening one.
a transfer to make
"You pay Marta back" · "Sam pays you back" · "Yusuf pays Marta back" — the settle-up screen's words, wherever a transfer is listed.
one made
"You paid Marta back" · "Sam paid you back" · "Sam paid Marta" — the list's title, the repayment's screen, its search and the saved toast.
a person
"You owe Marta" · "Marta owes you" · "You and Marta are settled up", under the figure on their page.
Do

Write each sentence as one message, with tags where the two halves are styled apart: <person>You</person> <verb>get back</verb>. French then says "Tu récupères".

Don't

Put a name next to a verb. "You" + "get back" came out "Toi récupères"; "Sam → Robin" named Robin to Robin in the third person and never said which way the arrow ran.

Totals

Group spending is a set of chips, one per currency — never summed into a single figure unless the group is in converted mode and has an explicit base currency. Adding euros to francs invents a number nobody agreed to.

One entry — its total, and each person's part

EUR 90.00

You paid €90.00 · you get back €60.00

  • A Ada €30.00 You get back €60.00
  • B Bob €30.00 Owes €30.00
total
Plain ink, no sign. It is a fact about the entry, the same for everyone in it — never the "you owe" red, which once greeted the person who had paid for the table.
your line
One whole sentence under the total — "You paid €90.00 · you get back €60.00", "Ada paid · you owe €30.00", "You’re not part of this expense". Only the half with a direction takes its balance ink.
split row
Name and share on one line, the share right-aligned; under the name, what the entry did to that person in words and in its balance ink. "You" on the reader's own row. Wraps rather than clipping.

A three-column table of person, share and signed balance needs 370px; a phone card has 328–358. It clipped the balances it existed to show, so the split is a list. Source: BigAmount, SplitRow in components/entries/detail-blocks.tsx.

Rules

Do €248.00 gets back

Word, icon, colour. Unsigned magnitude.

Don't +€248.00

Colour and a sign only. Illegible in greyscale, meaningless to a screen reader, and "+" means different things to different people.

Do

Keep amounts tabular-nums and right-aligned in any vertical list, so the decimal points line up.

Don't

Round for display, or drop trailing zeros. €7.10 is not €7.1, and a rounded total will not match the sum of its rows.
