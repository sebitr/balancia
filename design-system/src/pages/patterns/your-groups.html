The balance sits below the group name, behind a rule, not beside it. Beside it, a long group name and a figure compete for one narrow row and one of them loses — usually the name, to an ellipsis, which is the half a person needs to tell two trips apart.

One row per currency

A group that spans currencies shows each one. Summing them would require an exchange rate nobody chose, and the first card above is exactly the case that makes it obvious why: being owed €642 and owing ¥4,500 is not a single number, and pretending otherwise would be the one place in Balancia where a figure was invented.

Outstanding in any currency
Each non-zero balance, with its own word, icon and colour.
Whole units, and "≈" when that moved it
The list is scanned rather than reconciled, so a row drops the centimes — but says so: ≈ CHF 961 for 960.84, never a bare CHF 961 under a total reading CHF 960.84. A figure that is whole already carries no mark, and one under a single unit keeps its centimes: CHF 0.40, not ≈ CHF 0.
Square in every currency
A single "Settled up" — never a row of zeroes, and never €0.00.
No expenses yet
The words No expenses yet, never "Settled". A group nobody has recorded anything in has no balance, but it has not been settled either: settled is a result, and an empty group has not had one.

The sections, in order

One list from top to bottom, cut into sections by a small uppercase label. The label carries what every row under it has in common, so a row never repeats it in colour or size.

You owe · You're owed
Each group's own figure, largest first. These are the only groups that ask anything of the reader. Both are named for where the reader stands — French Tu dois and On te doit — and neither claims more than that: a group holding a debt in one currency and a credit in another, with no rate to net them, is filed under You owe and shows both figures, and the label is still true of it.
No expenses yet
Groups with no expense, income or repayment in them, in their own colours and with the words where an amount would be. Above the settled ones, because the next thing each needs is its first entry. When these are the only groups there are, the figure at the top says No expenses yet too, and the line under it "Nothing recorded in 1 group yet" — not "Settled up".
Settled up · 6
Groups where everything recorded has been paid back, dimmed one step, with "Settled" where an amount would be. Counted, and searchable once there are enough of them to need it.
Archived
One row that opens the rest, at the very end.

Where the figures come from

The same balance engine the group overview uses, called once per active group. That is more queries than a single aggregate would cost, and it is the right trade: the guarantee that every balance set sums to zero lives in one place, and a second implementation written for this screen would drift from it. Archived groups are not loaded — they are listed by name only.

Rules

Do

Lead with money. Head counts and currency modes are context; what somebody owes is the reason the app was opened.

Don't

Roll several currencies into one total, or colour a balance without also wording it. Both trade a true answer for a tidy one.
