Your groups

The screen people land on, and usually the only one they need. It answers the question they opened the app for — am I owed anything? — without making them pick a group first and find out.

Settled up

The balance sits below the group name, behind a rule, not beside it. Beside it, a long group name and a figure compete for one narrow row and one of them loses — usually the name, to an ellipsis, which is the half a person needs to tell two trips apart.

One row per currency

A group that spans currencies shows each one. Summing them would require an exchange rate nobody chose, and the first card above is exactly the case that makes it obvious why: being owed €642 and owing ¥4,500 is not a single number, and pretending otherwise would be the one place in Balancia where a figure was invented.

Outstanding in any currency
Each non-zero balance, with its own word, icon and colour.
Whole units, and "≈" when that moved it
The list is scanned rather than reconciled, so a row drops the centimes — but says so: ≈ CHF 961 for 960.84, never a bare CHF 961 under a total reading CHF 960.84. A figure that is whole already carries no mark, and one under a single unit keeps its centimes: CHF 0.40, not ≈ CHF 0.
Square in every currency
A single "Settled up" — never a row of zeroes, and never €0.00.
No expenses yet
The words No expenses yet, never "Settled". A group nobody has recorded anything in has no balance, but it has not been settled either: settled is a result, and an empty group has not had one.

The sections, in order

One list from top to bottom, cut into sections by a small uppercase label. The label carries what every row under it has in common, so a row never repeats it in colour or size.

Needs you · You're owed
Each group's own figure, largest first. These are the only groups that ask anything of the reader.
No expenses yet
Groups with no expense, income or repayment in them, in their own colours and with the words where an amount would be. Above the settled ones, because the next thing each needs is its first entry. When these are the only groups there are, the figure at the top says No expenses yet too, and the line under it "Nothing recorded in 1 group yet" — not "Settled up".
Settled up · 6
Groups where everything recorded has been paid back, dimmed one step, with "Settled" where an amount would be. Counted, and searchable once there are enough of them to need it.
Archived
One row that opens the rest, at the very end.

On a desktop

From lg (1024px) the screen sits beside the sidebar, as the desktop board "01 · Home" draws it. The same data and the same rows — nothing is mounted twice for the two widths — with the title drawn, the figures said in words, and from xl (1280px) a second column for what changed recently. Below lg nothing moves.

lg to xl
One column at the readable width every other screen keeps, max-w-3xl, centred beside the sidebar. "Recent in your groups" wraps under the groups.
xl up
Two columns, the groups and a 21.25rem column beside them, up to --app-content-max. The groups come first in the document, so a keyboard and a screen reader keep the phone's order.
Title row
"Your groups", today written out in the reader's language, and how many groups are open — neither settled nor archived, a group with nothing in it yet counted. The count is left out rather than said as zero. New group at the right; the position card's own two buttons step aside, since the sidebar holds Add expense.
Two currencies
Side by side at one size, half the card each, the debt first, each with its arrow and its word in the tone's ink — the owner's desktop direction. Two figures too wide for their halves wrap one under the other rather than run together. Three or more keep the lead figure and the rows of a phone, which is what #323 was written for. Why there is one figure per currency is still one tap behind the figure, never a standing line.
A row's figures
One line per currency, each said in words beside it — "€248 you are owed", "≈ CHF 62 you owe" — so the figure drops its sign. Still whole units, still "≈" when that moved it. The meta line counts the people beside the faces.
Settled and archived
Settled groups stay rows under their heading — the one-list rule above holds at every width. The archived groups' row is drawn as a pill, and opens the same rows under it.
Recent in your groups
The head of the inbox: its last five notifications, worded as the inbox words them, each one link to where the inbox's row goes, and "All notifications". Flat with hairlines, as the groups are — the position card stays the screen's one raised surface.
Not drawn
The board's hover preview of a group's last three expenses: this screen loads balances, not entries.
No groups yet
One column, centred, with room above it — the same title, the same card and the same Splitwise row, the card on its own surface.

Where the figures come from

The same balance engine the group overview uses, called once per active group. That is more queries than a single aggregate would cost, and it is the right trade: the guarantee that every balance set sums to zero lives in one place, and a second implementation written for this screen would drift from it. Archived groups are not loaded — they are listed by name only.

Rules

Do

Lead with money. Head counts and currency modes are context; what somebody owes is the reason the app was opened.

Don't

Roll several currencies into one total, or colour a balance without also wording it. Both trade a true answer for a tidy one.
